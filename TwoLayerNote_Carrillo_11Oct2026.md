The mock runs on one laptop. The floor does not. This note is the split.

## Near the kiosk

A watcher. It only needs to see that the lane has stopped answering: no button response after the first try, or a full freeze. It does not reset the register. Bianca’s order stays hers. Buttons first. If the whole system is frozen, shut it down and move the customer. Bringing it back is 5 to 10 minutes. The watcher does not do that job.

## With the attendant

The alert. One line: which lane, and when it stopped. That is the piece a person can read without standing at the frozen screen. Bianca is the outside owner. She is not the on-duty attendant in this design.

## How the alert travels

On paper, the watcher sends a short message to the attendant’s device. Lane number and time. Nothing else. No customer name, no items, no card. In the current mock both pieces are on one laptop, so the message does not leave the machine. A pass on that laptop is not a pass on the walk.

## What the notice-time test measures

Start at the freeze. Stop when the alert is on the attendant side. Pass is under one minute. That matches Bianca: a freeze that sits more than a minute is what backs the lane up, and customers usually wait 30 seconds to 2 minutes. The old three-second clock is retired.

## What this is not

Not a live register. Not a store install. Not a claim that the repository experiment met the charter target. I wrote the data and the rules, so that test stays a test of my own rules.
